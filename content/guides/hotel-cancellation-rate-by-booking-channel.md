Your hotel receives 50 reservations this month, but 10 guests cancel before arriving. How much should you worry?

Your **hotel cancellation rate** measures the percentage of reservations cancelled within a defined group of bookings. Tracking it separately for OTAs and direct bookings helps you understand which channels deliver reservations that actually turn into stays.

For independent hotels, guesthouses and villas, this can improve occupancy forecasting and pricing decisions.

## What Is Hotel Cancellation Rate?

Hotel cancellation rate measures the proportion of reservations cancelled before arrival.

**Cancellation Rate = (Cancelled Reservations ÷ Total Reservations) × 100**

For example, if 10 out of 50 reservations are cancelled:

10 ÷ 50 × 100 = **20% cancellation rate**

This means one in five reservations in that group was cancelled.

For accurate reporting, use the same booking population for both figures. One practical approach is to group reservations by scheduled arrival month and calculate the final cancellation rate after that month has passed.

Do not mix reservations created this month with cancellations from unrelated booking periods.

Also distinguish cancellations from no-shows. A no-show occurs when a guest fails to arrive without cancelling and should be tracked separately.

## Why Cancellation Rates Matter by Booking Channel

A property's overall cancellation rate can hide important differences between booking sources.

For example, OTA reservations may have different cancellation patterns from direct website bookings, depending on guest behaviour, booking conditions and the property's market.

Consider this illustrative monthly report:

| Booking Channel | Reservations | Cancelled | Cancellation Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Booking.com | 40 | 12 | 30% |
| Agoda | 20 | 4 | 20% |
| Direct website | 30 | 3 | 10% |
| Total | 90 | 19 | 21.1% |

*All figures are hypothetical.*

Although the property's overall cancellation rate is approximately 21%, Booking.com has the highest cancellation rate in this example.

That doesn't automatically mean the channel is performing poorly.

It may still generate valuable bookings, including reservations that would not otherwise reach the property.

The important question is how many reservations become completed stays and how much revenue those stays contribute.

## How to Create a Cancellation Report

Small properties can start with a spreadsheet rather than purchasing additional software.

### Step 1: Export Reservation Data

Collect reservations from your PMS, booking engine or OTA accounts.

Include the reservation ID, booking source, creation date, scheduled arrival date, room nights, rate plan and final status.

### Step 2: Choose a Consistent Period

For a final monthly cancellation report, group reservations by scheduled arrival month.

Wait until the reporting period has ended so that reservations have reached their final statuses.

### Step 3: Calculate Each Channel's Rate

Count cancelled reservations and total reservations for each channel.

Apply the same formula to every source.

Keep no-shows separate and avoid counting the same reservation twice after modifications.

### Step 4: Review Cancellation Timing

Record how many days before arrival each cancellation occurred.

A cancellation 30 days before arrival gives your property more opportunity to resell the room than one received on the arrival date.

Use a [booking pickup report](/guides/hotel-booking-pickup-report-guide/) to see whether new room nights replace cancellations for those future stay dates. Keep the snapshot dates and stay dates consistent.

### Step 5: Compare Revenue Impact

Reservation counts alone don't show the full financial impact.

A cancelled five-night villa booking can represent more lost room revenue than a cancelled one-night stay.

Track cancelled room nights and booking value alongside cancellation percentages.

## How to Reduce the Impact of Cancellations

The objective isn't necessarily to eliminate cancellations. Flexible booking conditions can make guests more comfortable reserving.

Instead, focus on reducing avoidable losses.

### 1. Make Cancellation Policies Clear

Show cancellation deadlines, refund conditions and payment requirements before guests confirm their reservations.

Avoid confusing or inconsistent policies across channels.

### 2. Review Your Rate Plans

Consider whether refundable and non-refundable options suit your property's demand patterns.

Stricter policies may reduce some cancellations but could also discourage bookings.

### 3. Monitor Late Cancellations

Review reservations cancelled close to arrival.

If certain dates frequently lose bookings at the last minute, investigate the reasons and whether your pricing or availability strategy needs adjustment.

### 4. Improve Direct Booking Communication

Send clear confirmations, arrival information and helpful pre-arrival messages.

Make it easy for guests to contact your property if their plans change.

### 5. Keep Inventory Updated

When a reservation is cancelled, ensure the room becomes available again where appropriate.

A correctly configured PMS and channel manager can help synchronize released inventory across supported channels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good hotel cancellation rate?

There is no universal target. Cancellation patterns vary by location, season, booking channel and cancellation policy. Compare your property against its own historical performance and relevant market data.

### Should hotels compare cancellations by reservation or room night?

Both can be useful. Reservation-based rates show how frequently bookings cancel, while room-night analysis better reflects the volume of accommodation affected.

### Does cancelling a reservation automatically refund the guest?

Not necessarily. Cloudbeds' documentation explains that changing a reservation's status to cancelled does not automatically refund payments. Refunds and cancellation charges require appropriate handling under the property's policy.

## Final Thoughts

A hotel cancellation report helps you understand the difference between reservations received and reservations likely to become completed stays.

Start by calculating cancellation rates for each booking channel, then examine timing, room nights and revenue impact.

**The goal is not simply fewer cancellations, but more predictable occupancy and better-informed revenue decisions.**
