## Begin with an ordinary working day

Before opening a software comparison, write down what your team does between breakfast and the last arrival. Taking reservations, answering questions, preparing rooms, collecting payments and updating availability are a better starting point than a vendor’s feature menu.

This is a general planning guide. Your local payment rules, property structure and booking mix may change what is appropriate.

## Your operational foundation

Most software discussions start with reservations and room inventory. Identify where the authoritative calendar lives and who can change it. Your team needs to understand that source of truth before adding more connections.

If you sell through multiple booking channels, examine how availability updates travel between them. If your website accepts bookings, confirm how those reservations enter the same inventory.

| Need | Tool category | Question to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manage stays | PMS | Can our team handle an arrival without a second spreadsheet? |
| Distribute inventory | Channel manager | What happens when an update fails? |
| Take direct reservations | Booking engine | Can a guest complete a booking on a phone? |
| Collect funds | Payments | Are our country, currency and refund workflow supported? |

## Add tools when a workflow needs them

Guest messaging, housekeeping and revenue tools can be helpful. They also introduce setup, cost and responsibility. First check what your current software already does and where it falls short.

### Guest communication

Write down the messages you repeat most often: directions, arrival times, access details and departure instructions. Check consent, channel support and how a person takes over from automation when something unusual happens.

### Housekeeping

A small team may need little more than a shared, dependable view of room readiness. A larger or distributed team may need assignments, inspections and maintenance escalation. Start with the actual handoff that is breaking down.

> Worth knowing: automating a confusing process can make mistakes happen faster. Agree on who owns each step before configuring an automation.

## Make the costs visible

List the subscription, onboarding, payment processing, integration and staff training costs. Ask which charges grow with rooms, properties, bookings or users. Compare like-for-like time periods and cancellation terms.

Use our [OTA commission calculator](/tools/ota-commission-calculator/) to understand one part of your distribution cost. It is an estimate, not a full profit calculation.

## Roll out one reliable workflow at a time

1. Document the current process and keep a backup of essential records.
2. Test your room setup, rates and bookings before going live.
3. Train the people responsible for each shift.
4. Check your first real reservations carefully.
5. Review what improved before adding another tool.

## A stack that fits your property

A six-room guesthouse should not inherit the complexity of a large hotel simply because a platform can support it. The useful stack is the one your team can run reliably, your guests can navigate easily and your business can afford as it grows.
