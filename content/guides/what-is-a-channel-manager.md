## One inventory, several shop windows

Imagine you run a six-room guesthouse. Guests can book on your own website and on two online travel agencies. Each website is a different shop window, but all three sell the same rooms.

A channel manager helps distribute availability, rates and restrictions between a central inventory and connected booking channels. When a reservation arrives, the available inventory needs to change everywhere else too.

This is a general educational guide, not a review of a particular product. Connection behavior varies by channel, vendor and setup.

## What happens when a guest books?

1. A guest reserves a room on a connected booking site.
2. The reservation reaches the connected management system.
3. Available inventory is reduced for the booked dates.
4. The new availability is sent to the other connected channels.
5. Your team checks that the reservation, rate and guest details arrived correctly.

The exact route can differ. In some setups the property management system is the source of inventory; in others, the channel manager manages it directly.

> InnStack Tip: Ask a vendor to demonstrate a booking, modification and cancellation across the channels you actually use. A successful new booking does not prove that every update is supported.

## Does it eliminate double bookings?

No system can guarantee that. A connection can be delayed, a room can be mapped incorrectly, or two guests can reserve the last room before an update finishes. Manual inventory changes can also create mismatches.

The useful question is how a system reduces risk and helps your team notice a problem. Look for visible connection status, failed-update alerts, a reservation activity log and clear support escalation.

### Calendar feeds and connected inventory

Calendar feeds generally exchange blocked or available dates. They do not necessarily carry the same rate, restriction and reservation detail as a more complete channel connection. Refresh intervals also matter. Ask what each connection supports rather than assuming all integrations behave alike.

## For a five-room property

Keep the setup simple. Write down each room type, how many units it contains and where it is sold. Confirm that one physical room has not accidentally become two separately sellable listings.

| Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Room mapping | A booking must reduce the right inventory. |
| Rate plans | Refundable and non-refundable rates may share rooms. |
| Restrictions | Minimum stays and arrival rules need a clear owner. |
| Cancellations | Released rooms should return to sale correctly. |

Choose one responsible person to monitor the first few days after launch. Keep a written fallback procedure for taking rooms off sale if a connection stops updating.

## A practical demo checklist

- Show one booking and trace it through the system.
- Change the dates and confirm both calendars update.
- Cancel it and confirm the room is released.
- Explain what happens when a connection is interrupted.
- Identify who handles channel support and room mapping.

The right setup should make an ordinary day easier to understand. Before you compare feature counts, compare how confidently your team can tell what is available tonight.

## Common questions

### Do I still need a PMS?

A PMS usually manages the operational record of the stay. A channel manager focuses on distribution. They may be separate products or parts of a larger platform. Read our [PMS and channel manager guide](/guides/pms-vs-channel-manager/).

### Should I connect every booking site?

Start with the channels that matter to your property. Each additional connection adds setup and monitoring work. Check its contribution and operating requirements before expanding.
