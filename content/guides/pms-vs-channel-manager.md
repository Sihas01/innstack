## Start with the job, not the acronym

A property management system, or PMS, is usually the operational workspace for reservations and stays. A channel manager distributes inventory and booking information between your property and external channels. These are distinct jobs even when one subscription includes both.

For a small operator, the distinction matters because two tools with overlapping marketing language may solve very different problems. This guide explains the general roles; it does not verify any vendor’s feature set.

## What a PMS is responsible for

Think about what your team needs at the beginning of a shift: today’s arrivals, guests in house, departures, room status and reservation notes. Those operational records typically belong in the PMS.

Depending on the product, accounting, housekeeping, payment processing or reporting may be included, optional or supplied through an integration. Ask what your subscription actually covers.

## What a channel manager is responsible for

A channel manager helps keep connected booking sites aligned with your sellable inventory. It can transmit availability, rates or restrictions and receive reservation updates, depending on the connection.

It is not enough for a product to list a channel logo. Confirm which data travels in each direction and which system owns the definitive inventory record.

| Everyday question | System to investigate first |
| --- | --- |
| Who is arriving today? | PMS |
| Are our booking sites selling the right inventory? | Channel manager |
| Can guests book on our own website? | Booking engine |
| Is the room ready? | PMS or housekeeping tool |

## For a small independent hotel

If one person handles reservations and the front desk, reducing duplicate entry may matter more than having the longest feature list. An integrated setup can simplify work, but only if its included components support your requirements.

> For a 5-room property: map a normal booking from confirmation to checkout. Mark every place you currently copy information. Those repeated steps are a useful starting point for your demo.

Ask what happens if you later replace one part of the stack. Can you export reservations and guest information? Does a separate channel manager still connect? Switching costs belong in the evaluation alongside the monthly fee.

## Questions to take into a demo

1. Which system owns room inventory and rates?
2. Where do staff manage modifications and cancellations?
3. Which integrations are included in the quoted price?
4. Can we export our data in a usable format?
5. Who investigates a booking that fails to arrive?

## Choosing the next step

If distribution is the main source of errors, start by examining channel connections and room mapping. If staff cannot see the same operational picture, start with your PMS workflow.
