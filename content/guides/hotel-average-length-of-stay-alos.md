Two hotels can achieve identical occupancy and room revenue while facing very different operating costs. One may welcome new guests every night, while the other accommodates guests staying several nights.

Average length of stay (ALOS) helps independent hotels, guesthouses and villas understand this difference and make better operational and revenue decisions.

## What Is Average Length of Stay in Hotels?

Average length of stay measures how many nights guests typically spend at a property per stay.

For room-based reporting, the formula is:

**ALOS = Total Occupied Room Nights ÷ Number of Completed Room Stays**

For example, if your guesthouse records 180 occupied room nights across 60 completed room stays:

180 ÷ 60 = 3 nights

Your average length of stay is three nights.

A room night represents one room occupied for one night. Two guests sharing a room for three nights generate three room nights, not six.

For reservations containing multiple rooms, count individual room stays rather than treating the entire reservation as one room stay.

This keeps the calculation consistent and prevents group bookings from artificially increasing your average.

## Why Average Length of Stay Matters

Occupancy measures how much accommodation inventory is being used. ALOS helps explain how that inventory is being used.

Longer stays can reduce the frequency of departure cleaning, check-ins, check-outs and room preparation.

Consider two hypothetical 10-room guesthouses operating over a 30-day month.

| Performance Metric | Guesthouse A | Guesthouse B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Occupied room nights | 240 | 240 |
| Occupancy | 80% | 80% |
| Average daily rate | $50 | $50 |
| Room revenue | $12,000 | $12,000 |
| Completed room stays | 120 | 60 |
| Average length of stay | 2 nights | 4 nights |

Both properties generate identical room revenue.

However, Guesthouse B handles half as many completed room stays.

Assuming each departure requires an additional $8 in turnover-related expenses, Guesthouse B could save $480 compared with Guesthouse A.

This simplified example excludes routine housekeeping and other operating expenses.

Longer stays do not automatically guarantee higher profitability, particularly when substantial discounts are required to attract them.

## How to Calculate and Track Hotel ALOS

Start by exporting completed reservations from your property management system (PMS).

For a simple monthly report, group completed room stays by checkout date and include their full stay duration.

Record the following information:

- Room reservation identifier.
- Actual arrival and departure dates.
- Number of occupied nights.
- Booking channel.
- Room revenue.

Exclude cancelled reservations and no-shows when calculating actual stay duration.

If you also track future reservations, calculate booked ALOS separately. Guests may cancel, extend or shorten their stays before departure.

Compare results by booking channel, room category and season.

For example, direct website guests might stay longer than guests arriving through a particular OTA. This information can help you evaluate which channels attract the most suitable demand for your property.

Avoid simply averaging monthly ALOS figures. Recalculate the overall average using total occupied room nights and completed room stays.

## How to Increase Average Length of Stay

The objective is to encourage profitable longer stays without unnecessarily rejecting valuable short-stay reservations.

### Offer Targeted Extended-Stay Rates

Consider offering a special rate for guests staying three, five or seven nights during quieter periods.

Before applying discounts, compare the expected additional revenue against the discount cost and potential operational savings.

Avoid heavily discounting dates that are already likely to sell at full price.

### Promote Experiences Beyond Accommodation

Guests may extend their visits when they discover additional activities near your property.

A boutique hotel could highlight local attractions, guided tours, cultural experiences or wellness activities on its website.

For a Sri Lankan guesthouse, a suggested three-day itinerary could encourage travellers to spend more time exploring the surrounding area.

### Encourage Extensions Before Checkout

When availability permits, offer existing guests the option to extend their stay.

A friendly message before departure can help guests discover available dates without searching for alternative accommodation.

### Review Short-Stay Demand

Short stays can still be valuable, especially for properties serving business travellers, airport passengers or weekend visitors.

Do not introduce restrictive booking conditions solely to increase ALOS.

Evaluate occupancy, room revenue and operating costs together before changing your strategy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a good average length of stay for a hotel?

There is no universal target. Suitable stay lengths depend on property type, location, season and guest demand. Compare your results with historical performance and similar properties.

### Is average length of stay the same as minimum length of stay?

No. Average length of stay measures guest behaviour. Minimum length of stay is a booking restriction that requires guests to reserve a specified number of nights.

### Does increasing ALOS improve RevPAR?

Not necessarily. RevPAR depends on room revenue and available room nights. Longer stays may improve occupancy or reduce turnover expenses, but excessive discounts can reduce room revenue.

## Final Thoughts

Average length of stay provides useful operational context that occupancy and ADR alone cannot reveal.

Track it consistently, compare booking sources and evaluate the financial impact of longer reservations.

**Focus on attracting profitable stays rather than simply increasing the number of nights guests book.**
